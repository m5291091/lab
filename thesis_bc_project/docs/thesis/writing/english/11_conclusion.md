# Chapter 11 Conclusion

## 11.1 Summary

This study designed a batch-based GPU execution framework for exact all-sources Betweenness Centrality on undirected, unweighted graphs and evaluated it on an NVIDIA GH200. The framework integrates source batching, block-based source processing, Hybrid BFS, Warp-Cooperative Accumulation, and Dual-Stream Execution. The evaluation examined performance, component effects, capacity, and numerical behavior. GPU_Opt is the main Unified Memory implementation, GPU_Opt_Pure is the device-only comparison, and GPU_Opt_Pure_Chunked is the source-sub-batched capacity variant. These implementations are memory-management variants of the common GPU execution framework, not 3 independently proposed methods.

## 11.2 Answers to the Research Questions

### 11.2.1 RQ1 Performance

RQ1 asked: On the four evaluated graphs, is the block-based GPU_Opt implementation with a fixed batch size of 512 faster than the graph-wise tuned third-party PathMerge implementation? The answer is `SUPPORTED`. On one GH200, fixed-`b512` block-based GPU_Opt was 3.17× faster on email-EuAll, 1.31× on roadNet-PA, 1.51× on roadNet-TX, and 1.45× on roadNet-CA. These median-to-median comparisons gave an overall range of 1.31×–3.17×. The result is limited to these 4 graphs, the evaluated GH200 configuration, and the evaluated PathMerge snapshot and configuration. PathMerge is a third-party implementation whose status as an official implementation by the original authors was not confirmed. It is an external comparator, not ground truth, and its upstream license remains unverified. The result does not generalize to PathMerge in general. The corrected 325557 graph was excluded from the RQ1 main performance comparison. The evidence does not attribute the RQ1 speedup to Unified Memory.

### 11.2.2 RQ2 Optimization Contributions

RQ2 asked: To what extent do Hybrid BFS, Warp-Cooperative Accumulation, Dual-Stream Execution, and the Block Kernel contribute to the observed performance? RQ2 is answered as `SUPPORTED_WITH_LIMITATIONS`. On the corrected 325557 graph, the descriptive main effects were 1.4767 for Hybrid BFS, 1.1012 for Warp-Cooperative Accumulation, and 1.5563 for Dual-Stream Execution. The corresponding Synthetic-4 aggregate values were 1.679, 1.066, and 1.391. This aggregate used mixed checkpoints because only the corrected 325557 result came from the separate corrected-input series. It was not a same-checkpoint four-graph remeasurement. Hybrid BFS and Dual-Stream Execution showed substantial observed main effects, whereas Warp-Cooperative Accumulation was graph-dependent. In particular, the email-EuAll result for Warp-Cooperative Accumulation did not show an improvement. The forced Block-versus-Shared comparisons were limited to roadNet-PA and roadNet-TX. These effects are descriptive main effects, not independent causal contributions, and they are neither additive nor assumed to be independent. No universal kernel-selection rule was established, and no unmeasured mechanism is assigned as their cause.

### 11.2.3 RQ3 Memory Scalability

RQ3 asked: On the evaluated corrected 325557 graph, how do the memory-management approaches of GPU_Opt, GPU_Opt_Pure, and GPU_Opt_Pure_Chunked affect the feasible batch size and the observed memory constraints? RQ3 is answered as `SUPPORTED_WITH_LIMITATIONS`. In targeted boundary validation, Pure succeeded at b4096 and failed at b8192 with a confirmed CUDA device-memory OOM. UM succeeded at b10240 and failed at b12288 under the host-memory cgroup limit. The per-configuration runtime record reported `classifier=none`, exit 137, and SIGKILL-compatible termination. Independently, the retained PBS epilogue recorded the host-memory cgroup OOM event. Chunked succeeded at the tested upper bound b16384 with `SUB_BATCH=6596` and `num_subs=3`. Each current corrected boundary condition had 1 observation. These observations concern the feasible batch range on one corrected graph and one GH200 configuration, not a performance ranking or general capacity limits. The capacity pressure arose from the working set that retained 10,418,856 bytes of per-source state for concurrent sources, not from the 45.35 MB input graph file. Source sub-batching groups source vertices and is not graph partitioning. UM is finite, Chunked does not guarantee unlimited capacity, and a larger batch does not imply higher performance.

### 11.2.4 RQ4 Correctness and Numerical Behavior

RQ4 asked: To what extent do the BC vectors produced by the proposed implementations agree with an independent reference and across different memory paths, and what numerical-representation and provenance limitations remain? Tier A is `SUPPORTED`. Full-vector comparisons between an independent Sequential reference and GPU_Opt passed on 3 small graphs. Tier B is `SUPPORTED_WITH_LIMITATIONS`. On the corrected 325557 input, all 10 inter-implementation comparisons among 6 vectors passed the predefined mixed tolerance, but Tier B is not independent-reference validation or ground truth. Across both tiers, all 13 comparisons had `MissingIndices=0` and `MismatchedElements=0`. None was byte-identical, and the evidence does not establish one cause for the numerical differences. The corrected input has consistent vertex ranges and CSR structure but retains its adjacency representation, including 87,442 self-loops and 866,924 duplicate ordered pairs. No independent validation established simple-graph semantics. Its provenance also remains limited because the original generation seed and complete upstream original are unconfirmed. The graph was excluded from RQ1 and used only for RQ2, RQ3, and Tier B. These retained properties are not presented as causes of the performance, capacity, or numerical results.

## 11.3 Contributions

This study makes four contributions in the following order. First, **Integrated GPU Execution Framework**: it integrates established GPU optimization elements into a batch-based exact BC framework for GH200. Second, **Performance Evaluation against a Tuned GPU Comparator**: it demonstrates performance within the evaluated scope against tuned third-party PathMerge. Third, **Component-Level Analysis**: it quantifies the H/W/A effects while preserving their graph-dependent limitations. Fourth, **Memory Scalability and Numerical-Boundary Analysis**: it evaluates memory capacity and numerical consistency separately, together with failure classes and provenance. The contribution is the integrated framework and its evidence-based evaluation, not the invention of the individual components or three independent proposed methods.

## 11.4 Final Remarks

The archive retains the `CORE_FAIL`, stress mismatch, and PathMerge difference from the former malformed `325557_3216152` input as historical invalid-input evidence; the current active conclusion does not use them. Within the evaluated graphs, GH200 configuration, implementation snapshots, and trial conditions, the common framework demonstrated both shorter runtimes against the evaluated tuned comparator and a broader feasible batch range across its memory-management variants. These results remain subject to numerical and provenance limitations. Independent full-vector references for the headline graphs, broader same-checkpoint component studies, repeated capacity-boundary trials, full-run memory telemetry, additional graphs, and other GPU platforms remain unresolved or unmeasured. Evaluating performance, components, capacity, and numerical behavior together makes the scope and strength of each result explicit. Thus, the study provides a restrained, evidence-based account of the integrated framework within the evaluated scope.
